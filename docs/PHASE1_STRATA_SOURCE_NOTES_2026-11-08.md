## What this says (to be checked by the Phase 1.2 profile, not yet a finding)

1. **The int8 tensor-core claim is the biggest lead.** Strata's own measurement on sm_120 puts int8 MMA at ~3.1x fp16's rate ("732 vs 233 measured", no unit given in the comment), and its MoE kernels (39% of Shadow's kernel time is MoE products) are built on it, with the weight decode hidden behind the MMA by a two-stage pipeline. Shadow's default MoE kernels run on fp16 MMA; the only int8 MoE path it has is llama.cpp's MMQ, a tie at the time (STATUS 46) and about to be a loss against the 21% faster fp16 gate/up the planner made (STATUS 61). So the open question is not "int8 or not" but **whether Shadow's kernels are decode-bound or MMA-bound**, which Nsight Compute can answer (needs the elevated shell).
2. **Work item shape.** 256 x 64 / 128 x 128 per work item picked by rows per expert, 16 warps a block, vs Shadow's 64-row tile x 16/32/64 tokens with MMA_K = 64: Strata's work items hold 64-128 routed rows per weight tile against Shadow's 16-64 tokens a group (so each decoded weight tile is reused over more rows; to be confirmed in the kernels), which is the lever the per-group cost model (STATUS 60-61: ~2.3 us fixed per group) says matters.
3. **DeltaNet** (5% of Shadow's kernel time): the 3-heads-per-thread + 8-token block staging is a known 1.41x on one card, worth a try as a cheap lever (under the 2026-11-08 rule a tie or better goes default).
4. **Attention**: int8 KV with int8 MMA exists in Strata as an opt-in (v3) - they did not make it default either; v2 (fp16 MMA, int8 KV) is its default.
5. **Strata streams at chunk 8,192; Shadow does 2,048-row sub-chunks inside a pass.** Not obviously a difference in cost (both hide the copy at 15k), but the fixed 2.3 us per group is paid per sub-chunk, so larger MoE batches would mean fewer groups (layer-wide MoE, STATUS 59).
